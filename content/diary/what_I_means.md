Ok hey two posts in one day. I have time on my hands and things to say.

The title is exactly what's been on my mind for the last few weeks, but especially those last few days. Just a year ago I felt like I had a grip on what it means to be me. Actually I felt like I had a grip on what it means to be _someone_. Now though, the basic question of "Who am I and what does it mean to be person?" gets harder and harder to answer. The more I learn about myself, about ourselves, about our system, the blurrier the meaning of "I" gets. Feeling a stable sense of self dissolve right in front of you is panic inducing.

When I switch, I never (or very rarely) feel like I am someone else. I am always Me. But who that "Me" is -- that's what changes. So who is that mystical "Me" when "I" am always someone else? It renders the meaning of "I" and "Me" and "mine" absolutely useless. And most of the people around me can't relate. Most of the people around me don't even know what the hell I am going through in the first place.

What compounds this feeling of "What the fuck"-ness is that we get realllyyy blurry. There's distinct headmates in the system who can front and sometimes do, but then there's who "I" am most of the time: The blurry pool of thoughts, emotions, beliefs and viewpoints of several people mixed with separate fragments and random memories that exist, but don't belong to someone in particular. They are just... there. And that's the """person""" people know us as. Maybe there is one sense of "This is me" in this body that's always ongoing. And that is tinted by various headmates, fragments, and floating memories. Sometimes someone comes to front and tints that sense of "Me" more than others in the system. And sometimes no one in particular is fronting and that's when we present as this pool of leaking emotions, thoughts and beliefs. That's what we call the Lavalamp and why we as a collective are called the Lavalamp System. Our sense of self is so fluid and dynamic that it reminded me of lavalamps. Great thing I love them.
